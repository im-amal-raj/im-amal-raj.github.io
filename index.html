How I Work

Building for Speed, Feel, and Freedom

I'm not writing corporate whitepapers; I vibe code tools that solve my own daily friction on Linux. Here are the simple principles that guide what I build:

01

Respecting the Hardware

I started tinkering on older machines where every megabyte mattered. Today it feels common for simple apps to consume hundreds of megabytes of RAM. I prefer lean, native binaries that sip battery and run silently.

02

Design as a Feature

I love Linux for user ownership, but desktop Linux has often treated visual polish as an afterthought. You shouldn't have to choose between software freedom and fluid interface feedback, good typography, and clean aesthetics.

03

Real Receipts Over Theories

Every optimization claim should be proven with real terminal logs, frame-time checks, and simple reproducible setups—not marketing buzzwords.
